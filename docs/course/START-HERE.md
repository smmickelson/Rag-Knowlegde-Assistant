# Start here

This is a template. It is a working, empty software project (the same shape a
real one has) with the course's paperwork folded into ordinary project files
instead of bolted on beside them.

Nothing here is named after an assignment, and there are no `stage-1/` folders,
because that is not how repositories are organized and you would have to
reorganize everything the first time you revised a document. Instead:
**[DELIVERABLES.md](DELIVERABLES.md) maps each stage to the files it grades.**
That's the only place stage information lives.

You can delete this `docs/course/` folder the day the semester ends and every-
thing still works.

---

## Your first hour

1. **Read [tracks.md](tracks.md)** and pick a track. This decides your language,
   how your tests run, and where your app gets hosted.
2. **Read [routine.md](routine.md).** It's the eight-step loop you'll run for
   every feature, 8-15 times. Learning it once saves you the whole semester.
3. **Read [`specs/00-example-feature.md`](../../specs/00-example-feature.md).**
   It's a finished spec, after questions and after user feedback. It shows you
   what you're aiming at better than any description can.
4. **Fill in [`AGENTS.md`](../../AGENTS.md) section 1**: three sentences about what
   you're building. Everything your assistant does gets better once that's there.
5. **Start [`docs/proposal.md`](../proposal.md) and
   [`docs/backlog.md`](../backlog.md).** Those two plus `AGENTS.md` and the Setup
   section of `README.md` are Stage 1.

## Two things worth knowing now

**Your plan is allowed to change.** The backlog you write in week 3 will not be
the backlog you have in week 13. That's expected. What gets graded is not that
you predicted correctly; it's that you recorded what changed and why.

**One habit matters more than the rest.** In the middle of the routine there's a
point where the AI has written the tests, explains them to you in plain English,
and stops, and nothing proceeds until you read them and say `approved`. That
pause is graded directly, and it's the thing that separates knowing what your
software does from hoping.
